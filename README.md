Comparative Analysis of Transformer Architectures for Masked Gene Expression Prediction

Abstract:
Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technologies have enabled the measurement of gene expression profiles at unprecedented resolution, providing critical insights into cellular heterogeneity, developmental trajectories, and disease mechanisms. The integration of machine learning (ML) with genomic data has become a promising approach to tackle the complexity and high-dimensionality of biological data. In this work, we perform a comprehensive comparative analysis of Transformer-based architectures for masked gene expression imputation in single-cell transcriptomic data. We evaluate four representative architectures: a lightweight custom GeneTransformer, a vanilla Transformer encoder baseline, (ii) a scBERTstyle model based on discretized expression bins, and (iv) an ScGPT-type model employing continuous value embeddings and a classification token for global cell representation. Our results demonstrate that all evaluated Transformer models consistently outperform discretization-based and lightweight architectures, achieving higher Pearson correlation, lower reconstruction error, and greater variance explained. Our findings provide practical guidance for model selection and architectural design in transcriptomics analysis.
